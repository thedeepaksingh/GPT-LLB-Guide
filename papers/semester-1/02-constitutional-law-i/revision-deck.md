# K-1002 — Flashcards and active-recall revision
**Created:** 2026-10-28 10:35 IST (UTC+05:30)  
**Status:** Revision aid; check current constitutional text and case law.

## 1. Flashcards
1. **What is constitutionalism?** — Limited, accountable exercise of public power under constitutional rules and rights.
2. **When was the Constitution adopted and when did it commence?** — Adopted 26 November 1949; commenced 26 January 1950, subject to the text's earlier commencement provisions.
3. **What is the Preamble's legal role?** — Part of the Constitution and an interpretive aid; not generally a freestanding cause of action.
4. **What is the basic-structure doctrine?** — Article 368 amendment power cannot be used to destroy the Constitution's basic structure.
5. **Does Article 368 require State ratification for every amendment?** — No; only specified categories require ratification by at least half of State legislatures.
6. **What is Article 12 for?** — Defines “State” for Part III, including Union/State governments, legislatures, local authorities and other authorities as stated.
7. **Is every government-funded entity automatically “State”?** — No. Apply the contextual instrumentality/agency tests and relevant precedent.
8. **What does Article 13 do?** — Addresses laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights; constitutional amendments are separately treated under Article 13(4) and the basic-structure doctrine.
9. **What is severability?** — Invalid provisions may be separated if the remainder can operate consistently with legislative intent.
10. **What is the eclipse doctrine?** — A doctrine associated with certain pre-Constitution laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights; exact operation depends on precedent and facts.
11. **Who is protected by Article 14?** — “Any person,” subject to the provision's scope.
12. **What is the traditional reasonable-classification test?** — Intelligible differentia plus rational relation to the law's objective; Article 14 also has an arbitrariness line of doctrine.
13. **Who holds Article 19 freedoms?** — Citizens; each freedom has its own constitutional restriction clause.
14. **Name the Article 19(2) grounds.** — Sovereignty and integrity; security of State; friendly relations with foreign States; public order; decency/morality; contempt of court; defamation; incitement to an offence.
15. **Can a restriction ground for one Article 19 freedom be imported into another?** — Not unless the relevant clause permits it; read the exact text.
16. **What are the three protections in Article 20?** — Protection against retrospective penal laws/greater penalties, double jeopardy in its constitutional terms, and compelled self-incrimination.
17. **Does Article 20 apply only to citizens?** — No; it uses “person” and applies according to its clauses.
18. **What did *Maneka Gandhi* contribute to Article 21?** — Procedure affecting life/liberty must be fair, just and reasonable; rights are not read in wholly isolated compartments.
19. **What did *Puttaswamy* decide?** — A nine-judge bench recognized privacy as a constitutionally protected right grounded in liberty and dignity.
20. **Is Article 21 absolute?** — No. Identify the right, State action, governing test, lawful limitations and relevant precedent.
21. **What is the difference between Article 22 arrest safeguards and preventive detention?** — Article 22 has separate clauses and exceptions; do not apply all ordinary-arrest safeguards identically to preventive detention.
22. **What does Article 23 prohibit?** — Trafficking, begar and other similar forms of forced labour, within the constitutional wording.
23. **What do Articles 25–28 cover?** — Freedom of conscience/religion, denominational rights, religious taxation limits and religious instruction in specified institutions.
24. **Are Articles 29 and 30 identical minority rights?** — No. Article 29(1) refers to a section of citizens with a distinct language, script or culture; Article 30 specifically protects religious and linguistic minorities' educational institutions.
25. **What does Article 32 provide?** — The right to move the Supreme Court to enforce Fundamental Rights and its specified writ/remedial powers.
26. **How is Article 226 different?** — High Courts may issue writs for Fundamental Rights and “for any other purpose”; jurisdiction and discretion still matter.
27. **What is habeas corpus?** — A writ testing the legality of detention.
28. **What is mandamus?** — A writ commanding performance of a public/legal duty, subject to legal limits.
29. **What is the relationship between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles?** — Part III rights are enforceable; Part IV principles guide governance. Both matter to constitutional interpretation; neither can be dismissed as irrelevant.
30. **What is the safest way to use a case in an answer?** — State the issue and holding/principle accurately, apply it, and avoid attributing more than the judgment supports.

## 2. Seven-day active-recall plan
- **Day 1:** Constitution's nature, history, salient features and constitutionalism.
- **Day 2:** Preamble, federalism, parliamentary system, separation of powers, amendment and basic structure.
- **Day 3:** Articles 12–13; write a short answer on State instrumentality and a short note on severability.
- **Day 4:** Articles 14–19; focus on the exact restriction grounds and the distinction between citizens/persons.
- **Day 5:** Articles 20–24; compare criminal protections, liberty/arrest safeguards and anti-exploitation provisions.
- **Day 6:** Articles 25–35; religion, cultural/educational rights, writs and special provisions.
- **Day 7:** Attempt the mock paper under timed conditions, then review errors and revise only weak topics.

## 3. Spaced repetition
Review a card on the day studied, the next day, after three days, after one week and before the exam. Mark each:
- **Green:** explain the rule and its limits without notes.
- **Amber:** remember the Article/case but miss a key element or exception.
- **Red:** cannot state the test accurately without notes.

Spend most time on amber/red concepts. In constitutional law, memorizing the Article number without its clause-specific test is not sufficient.

## 4. One-minute oral tests
- Explain why constitutionalism is more than the existence of a written Constitution.
- Explain the basic-structure doctrine without claiming an exhaustive list.
- Distinguish Article 13 review from Article 368 amendment review.
- Explain why Article 19(2) cannot be used as a general restriction clause for every freedom.
- Compare the traditional classification test and the arbitrariness line under Article 14.
- Explain the doctrinal change from *A.K. Gopalan* to *Maneka Gandhi*.
- Distinguish Articles 32 and 226.
- Explain why Article 21 is broad but not unlimited.
