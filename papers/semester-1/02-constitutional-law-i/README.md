## 4. Core approach for constitutional answers
For a problem question, use **issue → constitutional text → governing test → leading authority → application to facts → conclusion/remedy**. For an essay, define the doctrine, state the relevant articles, explain leading cases accurately, identify limits/criticisms, and conclude. A case name without the principle and its application earns limited value.

## 5. Critical distinctions
- Constitution vs constitutionalism.
- Written text vs conventions and constitutional practice.
- Preamble as an interpretive aid vs independently enforceable rights.
- Fundamental Rights vs Directive Principles.
- Article 12 “State” vs private actors; horizontal application depends on the right/doctrine.
- Article 13 review of laws vs Article 32/226 remedies.
- Restriction “by law” vs whether the restriction is constitutionally reasonable.
- Article 21 procedural protection and substantive fairness after *Maneka Gandhi*.
- Constitutional amendment under Article 368 vs the basic-structure limitation.
- Majority ratio vs obiter; historical case law vs current statutory text.

## 6. Current-law and source discipline
Use the latest official Constitution text and amendments. Article numbering is more stable than ordinary legislation, but doctrine evolves through judgments and amendments. State the provision and relevant judicial test rather than relying on a coaching mnemonic alone. The paper’s foundational syllabus is not a substitute for checking the latest constitutional amendments and full judgments.
