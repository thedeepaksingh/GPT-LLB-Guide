# K-1002 — Article map and leading-case revision table
**Created:** 2026-10-27 23:25 IST · **Status:** Revision aid, not a substitute for reading judgments.

## 1. Article map
| Topic | Main constitutional provisions | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| Constitution / Preamble | Preamble; Articles 1–4 for Union/territory; Articles 5–11 citizenship at commencement | Constitutional identity, purposes, and limits on what the Preamble does by itself |
| Amendment | Article 368 | Procedure; distinguish special majority and cases requiring State ratification; basic structure |
| State | Article 12 | Government, legislatures, local/other authorities; instrumentality tests |
| Inconsistent laws | Article 13 | Pre-constitutional laws, post-Constitution laws, severability/eclipsing; constitutional amendment distinction |
| Equality | Articles 14–18 | Equality before law, equal protection, discrimination, public employment, untouchability, titles |
| Freedoms | Article 19 | Six freedoms for citizens and the clause-specific grounds of restriction |
| Criminal protections | Article 20 | No retrospective penal liability/greater penalty, double jeopardy, self-incrimination |
| Life/liberty | Article 21 | Procedure established by law as interpreted through fairness, liberty and dignity cases |
| Arrest/detention | Article 22 | Arrest safeguards; preventive detention clauses separately |
| Exploitation | Articles 23–24 | Trafficking, forced labour, child employment as constitutionally stated |
| Religion | Articles 25–28 | Conscience, religious practice/denominations, religious taxation, religious instruction |
| Culture/education | Articles 29–30 | Cultural interests, admission protections, minority educational institutions |
| Remedies | Articles 32, 226 | Supreme Court remedy for Fundamental Rights; High Court writ jurisdiction for rights and other purposes |
| Rights limits / special application | Articles 31A–31C, 33–35 | Specific protections, modified application, martial-law context, parliamentary powers; use current text/case law |

## 2. Leading cases — high-yield doctrine
| Case | Topic | Principle for revision | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Kesavananda Bharati v State of Kerala* (1973) 4 SCC 225 | Amendment/Preamble | Basic-structure limitation on Article 368; Preamble is part of Constitution | No exhaustive fixed list of basic features |
| *Minerva Mills Ltd. v Union of India* (1980) 3 SCC 625 | Amendment / Parts III–IV | Limited amending power; constitutional balance between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles | State exact provision and holding in question |
| *S.R. Bommai v Union of India* (1994) 3 SCC 1 | Federalism / Article 356 | Federalism and secularism; review of President’s Rule proclamations | Multiple opinions; avoid oversimplification |
| *I.R. Coelho v State of Tamil Nadu* (2007) 2 SCC 1 | Ninth Schedule | Post-*Kesavananda* Ninth Schedule insertions may be reviewed under basic-structure analysis | Date and type of law matter |
| *State of West Bengal v Union of India* AIR 1963 SC 1241 | Federalism | Early discussion of Indian federal structure and Union powers | Read reasoning in context |
| *State of Madras v Champakam Dorairajan* AIR 1951 SC 226 | FR / DPSP | Early conflict between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles; historically important, later constitutional amendments affected reservation law | Do not use as a complete statement of current reservation doctrine |
| *Rajasthan Electricity Board v Mohan Lal* AIR 1967 SC 1857 | Article 12 | Interpretation of “other authorities” | Apply with later instrumentality tests |

| *Ajay Hasia v Khalid Mujib* (1981) 1 SCC 722 | Article 12 | Instrumentality/agency analysis | Apply subsequent clarifications and facts |
| *E.P. Royappa v State of Tamil Nadu* (1974) 4 SCC 3 | Article 14 | Arbitrariness and equality doctrine | Do not say all Article 14 issues use only the classification test |
| *Maneka Gandhi v Union of India* (1978) 1 SCC 248 | Article 21 / procedure | Fair, just and reasonable procedure; interconnected reading of Articles 14, 19 and 21 | Explain the actual holding and facts |
| *Shreya Singhal v Union of India* (2015) 5 SCC 1 | Article 19(1)(a) | Section 66A IT Act struck down; distinction among discussion, advocacy and incitement in speech analysis | Do not assume all online speech restrictions are invalid |

| *T.M.A. Pai Foundation v State of Karnataka* (2002) 8 SCC 481 | Article 30 | Minority educational institutions; regulatory framework | Multi-judge decision with detailed nuances |

## 3. Five-step answer framework
1. Quote or accurately paraphrase the relevant Article.
2. State the legal test in the relevant doctrinal context.
3. Cite one or two cases with the actual principle.
4. Apply the rule to the facts/question and state limits.
5. Give a reasoned conclusion and remedy.

## 4. Case-verification warning
This table is an exam-navigation tool. Before reproducing a precise ratio, quote, bench strength, later overruling or current status, consult the full official judgment or a reliable law report. Several cases contain multiple opinions and doctrines have developed over time.
