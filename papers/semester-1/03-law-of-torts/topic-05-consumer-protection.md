## 1. Purpose and statutory framework
The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 modernizes consumer rights, dispute redressal and enforcement in India. It addresses consumer status, goods and services, unfair trade practices, misleading advertisements, product liability, consumer commissions and the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA). It should be read with current rules, regulations and notifications. Do not copy pecuniary jurisdiction thresholds, limitation rules or procedures from the 1986 Act or an outdated summary.

**Official starting point:** India Code, https://www.indiacode.nic.in/ (locate the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and current subordinate legislation).

## 2. Who is a consumer?
The Act generally covers a person who buys goods or hires/avails services for consideration, including an approved user/beneficiary in the statutory circumstances. The definition excludes specified transactions, notably resale and commercial purpose, subject to the statutory explanation concerning use exclusively to earn livelihood by self-employment.

**Apply the definition in steps:**
1. Was there a transaction for consideration?
2. Did it concern goods or a service within the Act?
3. Is the claimant a buyer, hirer or qualifying user/beneficiary?
4. Does an exclusion apply, including resale/commercial purpose or a specific statutory exclusion?
5. Is the claim within limitation and brought before a competent forum?

Do not assume every person who receives a service is a consumer. Read the exact definition and exclusions in the current Act.

## 3. Goods and services
“Goods” and “service” have statutory meanings. Service is broad but includes exclusions, including a contract of personal service. The distinction between a **contract of service** (employment relationship) and a **contract for service** (independent service relationship) can matter. Courts examine substance, statutory wording and the nature of the relationship, not merely the label used in a contract.

*Indian Medical Association v V.P. Shantha* (1995) is a foundational interpretation under the Consumer Protection Act, 1986 regarding medical services. It remains historically important, but an answer on present law must analyze the 2019 Act, later authorities and the facts of the service arrangement rather than mechanically repeating an old proposition.

## 4. Deficiency in service
Deficiency refers to a fault, imperfection, shortcoming or inadequacy in the quality, nature or manner of performance required by law or contract, as defined in the Act. The claimant should identify the promised/statutory standard and show how the service fell short.

**Evidence examples:** contract/order, invoice, payment record, messages, service report, photographs, expert opinion where needed, complaint history and evidence of loss.

**Illustration:** A repair shop accepts payment to fix a machine, fails to perform the promised repair, and charges again without a valid explanation. If the customer qualifies as a consumer and the evidence supports the allegation, the conduct may amount to deficiency in service.

## 5. Defect in goods
A defect concerns a fault, imperfection or shortcoming in the quality, quantity, potency, purity or standard required by law or contract, under the statutory definition. The analysis asks what standard applied, what the goods were represented to be, how they failed, whether causation/loss is established, and what remedy is authorized.

## 6. Unfair trade practices and misleading advertisements
The Act defines unfair trade practices through specified forms of deceptive or unfair conduct. Misleading advertisements may attract statutory enforcement and other remedies. Avoid using “unfair” as a purely moral label; identify the specific statutory category and facts.

Potential issues include false claims about quality, performance, sponsorship, approval, price or benefits; concealment of material information; and deceptive representations. The exact statutory language and any relevant rules should be checked before writing section numbers.

## 7. Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA)
The 2019 Act establishes the CCPA with functions concerning consumer rights, unfair trade practices and misleading advertisements. Its statutory powers differ from the adjudicatory role of consumer commissions. An answer should distinguish regulatory/investigative enforcement by the CCPA from an individual consumer complaint seeking a remedy before a Commission.

## 8. Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions
The Act provides District, State and National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions. Jurisdiction, appeal routes, filing procedure and pecuniary limits are governed by the current Act and rules/notifications. These details can change, so verify them at the time of study and exam. Do not state old monetary thresholds as current without checking.

**Before advising a claimant, confirm:**
- territorial and pecuniary jurisdiction under the current framework;
- limitation period and whether any extension is legally available;
- who can file and whether representation is permitted;
- required documents and filing procedure;
- appeal period and forum;
- interaction with arbitration or other statutory remedies where relevant.

## 9. Remedies under the Act
Depending on the established ground and statutory powers, relief may include repair/removal of defect, replacement, refund, compensation, discontinuance of unfair trade practice, corrective advertisement, costs and other authorized orders. Product-liability actions may be available where the statutory conditions are satisfied. Never promise a particular remedy without checking the facts, statutory jurisdiction and current procedure.

## 10. Product liability
The 2019 Act includes a statutory product-liability framework addressing harm caused by a defective product or related service. Liability may involve a product manufacturer, product seller or product service provider depending on the statutory conditions. Analyze the relevant category, defect/deficiency, causal link, type of harm, statutory defences and exclusions. Do not assume that every product failure automatically produces product-liability damages.

## 11. Consumer law and other remedies
Consumer remedies may coexist with contract, tort or statutory remedies, subject to the governing statute and rules against double recovery or inconsistent proceedings. A deficient service may also raise contractual or tortious issues, but each cause of action has its own elements. State which forum and legal basis is being used.

## 12. Case law
**Indian Medical Association v V.P. Shantha (1995):** A leading decision interpreting medical services under the 1986 Act. Use it to explain the historical development of consumer treatment of medical services, then check how the 2019 Act and later case law apply to the facts.

For other cases, use only authorities verified against full judgments or reliable law reports. Record facts, issue, holding/ratio, later treatment and relevance. Do not rely solely on coaching summaries.

## 13. Worked problem questions

### Problem A: A customer pays a phone-repair shop, but the same defect remains and the shop refuses to provide the promised service.
**Answer:** Establish that the claimant is a consumer and the transaction is a service for consideration. Identify the promised repair standard, evidence of payment and service failure, and whether the failure amounts to deficiency under the Act. Check limitation and the competent Commission's current jurisdiction. Possible remedies depend on the Act and evidence and may include re-performance/repair, refund, compensation and costs.

### Problem B: An advertisement promises that a product cures a serious disease, but the claim is unsupported.
**Answer:** Identify the specific statutory category of misleading advertisement/unfair trade practice and the evidence of the representation. The CCPA may have regulatory powers under the 2019 Act; an affected consumer may also have a complaint or other statutory route depending on the facts. Do not assume the claim is automatically actionable without analyzing standing, statutory definitions and proof.

### Problem C: A product catches fire and injures the user.
**Answer:** Preserve the product, purchase proof, packaging, instructions, repair history and evidence of injury. Determine whether the problem is a product defect, warning/instruction failure, manufacturing/design issue, seller conduct or service deficiency. Analyze the statutory product-liability category, causation, harm, defences and any other applicable safety law. The result depends on evidence and the current statutory framework.

## 14. Practice questions

### Q1. Explain the concept of consumer under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.
**Answer plan:** State statutory definition; consideration; goods/services; users/beneficiaries; commercial-purpose and resale exclusions; self-employment livelihood explanation; apply to facts.

### Q2. Distinguish contract of service and contract for service.
**Answer plan:** Employment/control versus independent service; substance over label; relevance to statutory definition and exclusion; use an example; caution that the exact result depends on current law.

### Q3. Explain deficiency in service and remedies.
**Answer plan:** State definition; identify standard promised by law/contract; evidence; consumer status; competent forum; current jurisdiction/limitation; possible statutory remedies.

### Q4. Discuss product liability under the 2019 Act.
**Answer plan:** Identify manufacturer/seller/service provider; statutory basis; defect/deficiency; causation and harm; applicable defences/exclusions; remedy.

### Q5. Explain the role of the CCPA and consumer commissions.
**Answer plan:** CCPA regulatory/investigative/enforcement role; District/State/National Commissions adjudicate complaints; check current jurisdiction, procedure and appeals; do not conflate their functions.
