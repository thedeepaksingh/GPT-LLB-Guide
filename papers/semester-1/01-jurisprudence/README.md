The CCSU-hosted *LL.B. 3 Year (Six Semester) Course — Amended Syllabus (w.e.f. 2017–18)* states:

## 4. How to study jurisprudence
Jurisprudence is mainly conceptual and theoretical. Unlike a criminal statute paper, many topics do not have a single governing section number. For each school:
- identify its central question about what law is, where it comes from, and why it binds;
- state the thinker’s thesis in accurate terms;
- explain the components step by step;
- contrast it with another school;
- evaluate its explanatory power and limits;
- relate it carefully to legal institutions or an illustrative judgment;
- conclude by answering the wording of the question, not merely listing notes.

## 5. High-priority distinctions
Be ready to compare:
- jurisprudence as general/legal philosophy vs study of one legal system;
- analytical/positivist vs natural-law approaches;
- law as it is vs law as it ought to be;
- Austin’s sovereign command vs Kelsen’s normative hierarchy vs Hart’s primary/secondary rules;
- Savigny’s Volksgeist vs Maine’s historical evolution and status-to-contract proposition;
- Pound’s social engineering vs Duguit’s social solidarity;
- law in books vs law in action (realism);
- Marxist material/economic explanation vs purely formal/legal explanation.

## 6. Cases in a theory paper: use them accurately
Case law should be used as a short illustration of law, morality, authority, interpretation or social change—not as a replacement for the syllabus’s theorists. Useful starting points include:
- *Kesavananda Bharati v State of Kerala* (1973) 4 SCC 225 — the legal system’s approach to limits on constitutional amendment.
- *Maneka Gandhi v Union of India* (1978) 1 SCC 248 — interpretive development of procedural fairness under Article 21.
- *Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v Union of India* (2017) 10 SCC 1 — privacy and constitutional values.
- *Navtej Singh Johar v Union of India* (2018) 10 SCC 1 — dignity, autonomy and constitutional morality.
- *R v Dudley and Stephens* (1884) 14 QBD 273 — the relationship between necessity, morality and legal responsibility.

Before using any case in an exam, check that its facts and proposition support the point you make. Do not claim that a case directly adopted Austin, Hart, Pound or another theorist unless an authoritative judgment actually says so.
