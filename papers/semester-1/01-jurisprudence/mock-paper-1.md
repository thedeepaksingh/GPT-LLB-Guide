## Section A — Very short answers
**Answer all five questions. Each carries 4 marks. (5 × 4 = 20 marks)**

3. Explain Stammler’s phrase “natural law with variable content”.

## Section C — Long essays
**Attempt any three questions. Each carries 20 marks. (3 × 20 = 60 marks)**

9. Define jurisprudence and discuss its nature, scope and importance. Compare at least two definitions.
10. Explain the revival of natural law with special reference to Stammler and Kohler.
11. Critically examine Pound’s theory of social engineering. Explain the classification and reconciliation of interests.
12. Discuss American Modern Legal Realism. Explain the contributions of Holmes, Llewellyn and Frank and assess its limitations.
13. Explain the Marxist economic approach to law. Discuss material relations, class, law and the danger of economic determinism.

## Self-marking rubric
Award 0–2 points for each category and scale the score to the question’s marks:
1. Answers every part of the question.
2. Defines the central concept correctly.
3. Explains all essential elements, not only labels.
4. Distinguishes thinkers and theories accurately.
5. Includes a relevant and properly described example/authority where useful.
6. Gives substantive criticism or counter-argument.
7. Uses logical headings and coherent paragraphs.
8. Concludes by answering the question directly.

**Section A:** one correct definition plus clear explanation and one distinguishing feature/example where useful.  
**Section B:** define the issue, explain major elements, make a relevant comparison/criticism, conclude.  
**Section C:** introduction; structured doctrine; contextual explanation; critical evaluation; relevant illustration; direct conclusion.
