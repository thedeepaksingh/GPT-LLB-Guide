## Applicability decision

For the stated admission session (2024–25 or later) and regular exam, this repository follows **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS), paper K-1006**. CCSU's January 2026 LL.B. Semester I programme identifies K-1006 as “Law of Crimes – I Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023 (BNS) (Start w.e.f. 2024-25)” and labels K-1004 (Indian Penal Code) for ex/back-paper students only. Always follow the current notice issued for your own examination.

The BNS came into force on 1 July 2024, subject to the notified exception for BNS section 106(2). This paper is about **substantive criminal law**. Criminal procedure is principally governed by the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS); evidence by the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (BSA). Do not treat the three Acts as interchangeable.

## Statutory anchors (BNS)

Use the current official Act text as the authority. The following section ranges are navigational anchors, not a substitute for reading the actual statutory language:

- Sections 1–3: preliminary provisions, definitions and general explanations.
- Sections 4–13: punishments and rules relating to punishment.
- Sections 14–44: general exceptions and private defence (read each provision closely).
- Sections 45 onward: abetment; sections 61–62 address criminal conspiracy and attempt.
- Sections 63 onward: sexual offences; later chapters address offences affecting the human body, State, public tranquillity, property, documents and reputation. Verify the exact section for each offence from the current Act before citing it in an answer.

## Scope and quality note

These are structured exam-preparation notes, not legal advice. The topics reflect the broad criminal-law core and should be mapped against the exact K-1006 syllabus/annexure and IEC faculty instructions before calling coverage final. Case-law propositions are summaries; consult the reported judgments for precise ratio, facts, subsequent treatment and statutory context. Do not mechanically replace IPC section numbers with BNS section numbers: the statutory text and wording must be checked offence by offence.

## Exam-answer method

For a problem question: (1) identify the precise issue/offence; (2) state the BNS provision and ingredients; (3) state the relevant mental element and causation, where required; (4) apply each ingredient to the facts; (5) consider exceptions/defences; (6) distinguish evidentiary gaps from substantive elements; and (7) conclude provisionally on liability and possible punishment. For an essay, use definition, statutory framework, ingredients, leading authority, critical analysis, illustration and conclusion.
